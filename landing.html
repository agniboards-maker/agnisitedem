Care Instructions+

To Clean Your Agni Board

Cleaning the nails

  1. Spray a small amount of sanitizer onto a clean cloth. Don’t spray it directly onto the nails.
  2. Wipe the nails down gently.

Cleaning the wood

  1. Use a dry rag to wipe the wood.
  2. Don’t use sanitizer or any liquid on the wood. It can damage the protective finish.

Tip: Clean the nails after each use and keep the board dry.

Keep your board out of direct sunlight, since UV rays can fade its colour. Store it where pets and children can’t reach it.

FAQ

How does it work?+

Agni is a modern take on the Indian bed of nails, an ancient tool for easing inner tension. You stand on it, and the nails press into your feet across a large number of acupoints. It works on the same principles as acupressure, without piercing the skin. Most people feel some initial discomfort that gives way to warmth and muscle relaxation within a few minutes.

What are the benefits?+

Everyone responds differently, but these are the things people tell us about most:

  • Unwinding after a long day
  • Winding down for sleep or relaxing before bed
  • Easing tension in a tight back, neck, and shoulders
  • Building a daily ritual that brings calm and peace
How do you use it?+

The most popular times are before sleep and right after you wake up. Put the Agni board on the floor, then either stand on it or sit in a chair with your feet on the board and massage them gently. Some people also meditate while standing on it.

How do I get past the first few minutes?+

The first few minutes are meant to be uncomfortable, and that’s how the nails get to work. Some people call that friction a kind of tapas, the heat you feel when something is hard. Acupressure asks you to move through a bit of sting and come back to yourself. You feel it, then let it go. After a few minutes, most people find the sting turns into warmth.

Pro tips

  • Step on and off slowly. Shifting your weight around is what stings most.
  • Breathe slowly and let your body settle in, rather than bracing against the nails.
  • Start with 5 to 10 seconds and build up to a few minutes.
  • Bare feet work best, but socks are fine during your first few sessions while you get used to it.
Do the nails hurt?+

Yes, a little, but mostly at the start. The sting is strongest in the first seconds and fades as you settle in. Some people also notice the pressure feels stronger when they’re tense, so relaxing your body can make the sensation easier to handle.

The first few seconds usually stings, then tingling turns into warmth, and many people feel calm within a few minutes. Redness or small marks on the skin can show up after a session. If your feet feel sore the next day, shorten your sessions and take a rest day before you use it again.

Which intensity should I choose?+

Just like a mattress’s firmness or the pressure of a massage, acupressure doesn’t suit everyone the same way. Starting too hard can make it harder to stick with the habit, which is why we offer two intensity levels.

Level 1 is the easiest place to begin. You get a gentle mix of initial sensation and a deep sense of release, and it suits anyone new to acupressure.

Level 2 is for people who want more. With fewer nails, there’s more initial discomfort, so you’ll need to push through it to get the deeper release.

What are acupoints?+

Acupoints, sometimes called pressure points, are specific spots on the body that acupressure works on. In traditional practice, pressure on these points is linked to relaxation and easing tension.

They’re spread across the body, including the feet. The Agni nails press into many of them at once, so you don’t need to find them yourself to feel the effect.

Is this the same as acupuncture?+

No. Acupuncture uses thin needles that go into the body. Acupressure uses pressure instead. With Agni, the nails press against your feet without piercing the skin.
